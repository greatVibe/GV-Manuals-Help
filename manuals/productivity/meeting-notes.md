# Meeting Notes

Use this guide when preparing, taking, or sharing notes from a meeting, review, workshop, or decision discussion.

Good meeting notes help people remember what was agreed, what still needs work, and who is responsible for each next step.

## Before The Meeting

Before the meeting starts, prepare a simple note structure.

Include:

- Meeting title.
- Date and time.
- Attendees.
- Purpose of the meeting.
- Agenda items.
- Space for decisions.
- Space for action items.
- Space for questions or risks.

If the meeting has documents, links, or files to review, list them near the top so they are easy to find later.

## Capture The Purpose

Write down why the meeting is happening.

Examples:

- Review a document.
- Make a decision.
- Plan upcoming work.
- Resolve an issue.
- Share progress.
- Confirm next steps.

A clear purpose helps keep the notes focused and makes it easier for someone who missed the meeting to understand the outcome.

## Record Decisions Clearly

When a decision is made, write it as a decision, not as general discussion.

For each decision, capture:

- What was decided.
- Who agreed to it.
- Any conditions or assumptions.
- Whether the decision is final or needs more review.
- The date of the decision.

Avoid burying decisions inside long paragraphs. Keep them easy to scan.

## Track Action Items

Action items should be specific enough for someone to complete them without guessing.

Each action item should include:

- The action to complete.
- The owner.
- The due date, if known.
- Any dependency.
- The expected output.

Use active wording such as prepare, send, review, update, confirm, or decide.

## Separate Notes From Outcomes

Discussion notes and outcomes are useful in different ways.

Discussion notes explain context. Outcomes show what changed because of the meeting.

Keep these separate where possible:

- Discussion points.
- Decisions.
- Action items.
- Open questions.
- Risks or blockers.
- Follow-up topics.

This makes it easier to review the notes later.

## Capture Open Questions

If a question cannot be answered during the meeting, record it clearly.

For each open question, note:

- The question.
- Who should answer it.
- Why it matters.
- When an answer is needed.
- Whether it blocks a decision or task.

Open questions should not disappear into general notes. They need follow-up.

## Record Risks And Blockers

If something may delay work or affect the outcome, record it as a risk or blocker.

Useful details include:

- What the risk or blocker is.
- What it affects.
- Who can help resolve it.
- Whether it needs urgent attention.
- What the next step is.

This helps the team avoid repeating the same discussion later.

## Keep Notes Neutral

Meeting notes should be factual and neutral.

Use clear language and avoid personal commentary. Focus on:

- What was discussed.
- What was decided.
- What needs to happen next.
- What remains unclear.

If a topic is sensitive, record the outcome carefully and avoid unnecessary detail.

## After The Meeting

After the meeting, clean up the notes while the discussion is still fresh.

Check that:

- Decisions are easy to find.
- Action items have owners.
- Due dates are included where possible.
- Open questions are listed.
- Draft or unclear wording has been corrected.
- Links or attachments still work.
- The notes are shared with the right people.

If the notes include anything uncertain, mark it clearly or ask for confirmation before sharing widely.

## Sharing Meeting Notes

When sharing meeting notes, include a short message that explains what the recipient should do.

Examples:

- Review the decisions.
- Confirm your action items.
- Add missing context.
- Reply with corrections by a certain date.
- Use the notes as the reference for next steps.

Do not assume everyone will read the full notes. Put the most important outcomes near the top.

## Quick Checklist

Before considering meeting notes complete:

- The purpose is clear.
- Decisions are listed separately.
- Action items have owners.
- Due dates are included where possible.
- Open questions are visible.
- Risks or blockers are recorded.
- Notes are factual and neutral.
- The right people received the notes.
- Any corrections or confirmations are tracked.
