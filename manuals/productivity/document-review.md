# Document Review

Use this guide before sharing a document, report, spreadsheet, or exported file with another person.

A document review should confirm that the content is accurate, complete, easy to read, and appropriate for the intended audience.

## Before You Start

Confirm:

- The purpose of the document.
- Who will read it.
- Whether the document is a draft or final version.
- Whether any sections need approval before sharing.
- Whether the file includes data, assumptions, or recommendations that should be checked carefully.

If the audience or purpose is unclear, ask before editing or sharing the document.

## Content Checks

Review the document for:

- Missing sections or incomplete notes.
- Outdated facts, dates, names, figures, or links.
- Placeholder text that should be removed.
- Repeated paragraphs or duplicated sections.
- Statements that are too vague to act on.
- Claims that need evidence or a source.

When something looks uncertain, leave a note or ask for confirmation instead of guessing.

## Formatting Checks

Check that the document is easy to scan.

Look for:

- Clear headings and section order.
- Consistent bullet style.
- Consistent numbering.
- Tables that fit on the page.
- Images or charts that are visible and labelled.
- Enough spacing between sections.
- No broken layouts after export.

For long documents, review the exported version as well as the editable version.

## Link And Reference Checks

Before sharing, open important links and confirm they work.

Check that:

- Links go to the intended page or file.
- Reference names match the document text.
- Attachments are included when mentioned.
- Cross-references point to the right section.
- File names are clear and current.

Do not assume a link works because it worked in an older version.

## Data And Calculation Checks

If the document includes numbers, tables, or spreadsheet results, confirm:

- Totals match the supporting detail.
- Dates and time periods are clear.
- Units, currencies, or percentages are labelled.
- Rounded values are not misleading.
- Charts match the data they describe.
- Any assumptions are visible to the reader.

Ask for review when figures affect decisions, reporting, customer communication, or financial outcomes.

## Sensitive Information Check

Before sharing externally, check for information that should not be public.

Remove or confirm approval for:

- Personal contact details that are not meant to be shared.
- Private customer information.
- Internal notes or comments.
- Draft-only feedback.
- Credentials, access links, or private tokens.
- Commercial details that are not approved for release.

When in doubt, stop and ask before sharing.

## Version Check

Confirm you are sharing the correct version.

Check:

- The file name.
- The last edited date.
- The intended audience.
- Whether tracked changes or comments should remain visible.
- Whether the document should be shared as an editable file or an exported file.

For final documents, export or save a clean copy before sending it externally.

## Review Workflow

A safe review workflow is:

1. Confirm the purpose and audience.
2. Review content for accuracy and completeness.
3. Check formatting and layout.
4. Verify links, references, and attachments.
5. Check any numbers, tables, or calculations.
6. Remove draft notes and sensitive information.
7. Export or save the final version.
8. Do one final read-through before sharing.

## When To Ask For Help

Ask for review when:

- The document will be shared externally.
- The document includes customer, financial, legal, or operational details.
- The content affects a decision or commitment.
- The layout changes after export.
- You are unsure whether information is appropriate to share.

## Quick Checklist

Before sharing a document:

- Confirm the audience and purpose.
- Remove placeholders and draft notes.
- Check dates, names, figures, and links.
- Review formatting after export.
- Confirm attachments are included.
- Remove sensitive information.
- Ask for review when the impact is broad or unclear.
