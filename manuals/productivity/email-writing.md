# Email Writing

Clear email helps people make decisions faster. A useful message says what is needed, why it matters, and what should happen next.

This guide covers practical ways to write email for work, support, operations, and everyday communication.

## When to use this guide

Use this guide when you need to:

- Ask someone to make a decision.
- Share a status update.
- Request information or files.
- Follow up on an unresolved item.
- Summarize a discussion or outcome.
- Send a customer-facing or partner-facing message.

## Start with the outcome

Before writing, decide what the email needs to achieve.

Ask:

- What does the reader need to know?
- What action do I want from them?
- Is there a deadline?
- What context is necessary?
- What can be left out?

If there is no clear outcome, do not start drafting yet. Clarify the purpose first.

## Use a specific subject line

A good subject line lets the reader understand the message before opening it.

Prefer:

- Action needed: approve invoice by Friday
- Status update: March report is ready for review
- Question: confirm attendee list for Tuesday meeting
- Follow-up: missing figures for budget worksheet

Avoid vague subjects such as:

- Quick question
- Update
- Important
- Following up
- Request

## Put the ask near the top

If the email needs action, say so in the first few lines.

A useful opening structure is:

1. State the request.
2. Give the reason.
3. Name the deadline or desired timing.

Example:

Please review the attached draft and send any changes by Thursday afternoon. I need the final version ready before the Friday planning meeting.

## Keep context short

Readers need enough context to act, but not every detail you know.

Include:

- The current situation.
- The decision or action needed.
- Any constraints, dates, or dependencies.
- Links or attachments the reader needs.

Leave out:

- Full history unless it changes the decision.
- Side issues that belong in a separate message.
- Internal notes that are not relevant to the reader.
- Repeated explanations already covered in the thread.

## Make long emails scannable

For messages longer than a few short paragraphs, use headings or bullets.

Useful sections include:

- Summary
- Action needed
- Background
- Options
- Recommendation
- Deadline
- Attachments

Keep each section focused. If a section grows too long, split it or move detail into an attachment.

## Match the tone to the relationship

Use a tone that is calm, direct, and respectful.

For internal updates, be concise and clear.

For customer or partner messages, add a little more context and avoid shorthand.

For sensitive topics, be factual and avoid blame. Describe the situation, the impact, and the next step.

## Use plain language

Prefer words that are easy to understand.

Use:

- Please review
- The report is ready
- We need a decision by Friday
- I recommend option two

Avoid unnecessary phrases such as:

- Please be advised
- As per our previous correspondence
- At your earliest convenience
- Kindly action the below

Plain language is not casual. It is easier to act on.

## Be clear about deadlines

If timing matters, use a specific date or time.

Prefer:

- Please send feedback by 3 PM on Thursday.
- I need approval before the end of the day on Friday.
- The file is due before Monday's client meeting.

Avoid:

- Soon
- ASAP
- When you get a chance
- By close of business, unless your team uses that term consistently

## Handle attachments and links carefully

When sending files or links, explain what they are and what the reader should do with them.

Example:

I attached the revised spreadsheet. Please check the totals on the Summary tab and confirm whether the numbers match your records.

Before sending, confirm that:

- The attachment is included.
- The file name is clear.
- The link opens for the intended audience.
- The recipient has the access they need.
- The email does not include unrelated or private material.

## Follow up without adding noise

A good follow-up reminds the reader what is needed and why.

Example:

Following up on the approval request below. Please confirm by Wednesday afternoon so the final file can be sent on schedule.

Do not resend the same message repeatedly without adding useful context. If the matter is urgent, state the consequence of missing the deadline.

## Before sending

Review the email for:

- Clear subject line.
- Request or purpose near the top.
- Correct recipients.
- Correct attachments or links.
- Specific deadline, if needed.
- No private or unrelated information.
- Professional tone.
- Spelling and formatting.

## Quick template

Subject: Action needed: [decision or task] by [date]

Hi [name],

Please [action needed] by [date/time].

This is needed because [brief reason].

Context:

- [Important detail]
- [Important detail]
- [Attachment or link, if relevant]

My recommendation is [recommendation], because [reason].

Thanks,
[Your name]

## Key takeaway

A strong email makes the next step obvious. Put the ask early, keep context short, and make deadlines specific.
