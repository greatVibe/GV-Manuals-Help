# Recurring Reviews

A recurring review is a planned check-in that keeps work, files, tasks, decisions, and follow-up actions accurate over time. Good reviews prevent small unresolved items from becoming stale, unclear, or forgotten.

This guide explains how to run recurring reviews for productivity work such as shared documents, spreadsheets, project summaries, meeting notes, approvals, issue reports, status updates, and action lists.

## When to use this guide

Use this guide when you need to:

- Review open tasks on a regular schedule.
- Check whether shared files are still current.
- Confirm that decisions and approvals have been recorded.
- Close completed follow-up actions.
- Find stale issue reports or blockers.
- Prepare a reliable status update.
- Keep project summaries aligned with recent work.
- Confirm that owners and due dates are still correct.

A recurring review is most useful when the same work area changes over time and several people depend on the latest state.

## Choose a review rhythm

Pick a rhythm that matches the pace of the work.

Common review rhythms include:

- Daily: active work, urgent issues, live handoffs, and short-term blockers.
- Weekly: task lists, project summaries, meeting action items, and team status updates.
- Monthly: recurring reports, archive checks, decision records, and larger planning documents.
- Before sharing: files, PDFs, presentations, and external updates.
- After approval: approved documents, exported files, and final summaries.

Avoid reviewing everything every day. A review should be frequent enough to catch problems, but not so frequent that people stop taking it seriously.

## Start with the purpose

Every recurring review should have a clear purpose.

Examples:

- Confirm that all open actions have an owner and next step.
- Check that the project summary reflects the current state.
- Verify that the shared folder contains only current files.
- Review unresolved feedback before the next approval round.
- Confirm that issue reports have enough detail to be acted on.

If the purpose is unclear, rewrite it before starting the review.

## Prepare the review list

Before the review, collect the items that need attention.

Useful review inputs include:

- Open follow-up actions.
- Recent meeting notes.
- Current task list.
- Shared documents and spreadsheets.
- Latest status update.
- Unresolved comments or feedback.
- Recent approvals or decisions.
- Open issues and blockers.
- Previous review notes.

Keep the list focused. A recurring review should not become a full archive search unless that is the stated purpose.

## Review each item the same way

Use a consistent set of questions for each item.

Ask:

- Is this still needed?
- Who owns it?
- What is the next step?
- Is the due date still realistic?
- Is anything blocked?
- Has the result been recorded?
- Does anyone else need to know?
- Can this be closed, deferred, or cancelled?

Consistent questions make reviews faster and reduce the chance that important items are missed.

## Update the record during the review

Do not rely on memory after the review. Update the source record while decisions are fresh.

Useful updates include:

- Marking completed items done.
- Adding missing owners.
- Updating due dates.
- Recording blockers.
- Moving deferred work to a later list.
- Linking to the latest file or summary.
- Adding a short result for closed actions.
- Removing duplicate or obsolete items.

A review is only useful if the reviewed information becomes easier to trust afterward.

## Keep notes short

Recurring review notes should be brief and practical.

Prefer:

- Done: PDF exported and shared with reviewers on April 10.
- Blocked: Waiting for final spreadsheet total from the reporting team.
- Deferred: Moved to next week's planning list.
- Cancelled: No longer needed because the report was replaced.

Avoid long background explanations unless they are needed to understand the next step.

## Separate review outcomes from discussion

A recurring review may include discussion, but the saved record should focus on outcomes.

Capture:

- What changed.
- What remains open.
- Who owns the next step.
- What is blocked.
- What was closed.
- What needs approval.

Do not copy long conversation threads into the review record. Summarize only the information needed for future readers.

## Watch for stale work

Recurring reviews are useful for finding work that has stopped moving.

Signs that an item may be stale include:

- No owner.
- No due date where timing matters.
- Repeated status of waiting with no blocker detail.
- A linked file that has been replaced.
- A decision that was made but not recorded.
- A task that remains open after the result already exists.
- A blocker that no longer has a clear next step.

For stale items, either refresh the owner and next step or close the item with a short reason.

## Protect private information

Recurring reviews often collect information from many sources.

Do not include:

- Passwords or access tokens.
- Sensitive personal information.
- Private customer details unless required and approved.
- Internal notes that should not be shared.
- Links that readers should not be able to access.
- Long message history unrelated to the review outcome.

If sensitive context is needed, summarize the safe action and keep restricted details in the appropriate secure location.

## Recurring review template

Use this template for a simple recurring review.

Review name:
[Name of work area]

Review date:
[Date]

Review rhythm:
[Daily, weekly, monthly, before sharing, after approval, or other]

Purpose:
[What this review is meant to confirm]

Items reviewed:
[List or link to reviewed items]

Closed items:
[Items completed, cancelled, or no longer needed]

Open actions:
[Actions that still need work]

Blockers:
[Blocked items and what is needed next]

Decisions or approvals needed:
[Items awaiting a decision]

Next review:
[Date or timing]

## Example recurring review

Review name:
Monthly summary review

Review date:
April 10, 2026

Review rhythm:
Weekly until final approval

Purpose:
Confirm that the monthly summary is ready for final sharing.

Items reviewed:
Draft summary, spreadsheet totals, approval notes, export checklist, open follow-up actions.

Closed items:
- File name updated to match the naming standard.
- Duplicate rows removed from the spreadsheet.
- Review comments from April 8 resolved.

Open actions:
- Add the approval date to the final summary. Owner: Project lead. Due: Before export.
- Export the approved summary as a PDF. Owner: File owner. Due: After approval.

Blockers:
- Waiting for confirmation that the April total includes hidden rows.

Decisions or approvals needed:
- Final approval needed before external sharing.

Next review:
Before final export.

## Quick checklist

Before finishing a recurring review, confirm that:

- The review had a clear purpose.
- The right items were checked.
- Completed items were closed.
- Open actions have owners.
- Due dates are included where timing matters.
- Blockers explain what is needed next.
- Decisions and approvals are separated from actions.
- Stale items were refreshed, deferred, cancelled, or closed.
- Private information was not exposed.
- The next review timing is clear.

## Key takeaway

A recurring review keeps shared work trustworthy. Use a clear rhythm, review the same questions each time, update the source record during the review, close what is done, refresh what is stale, and leave the next step easy to see.
