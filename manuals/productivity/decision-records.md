# Decision Records

A decision record explains what was decided, why it was decided, and what should happen next. It gives teams a clear history without requiring everyone to search through messages, meetings, documents, or comments.

This guide explains how to write practical decision records for productivity work, shared documents, reviews, approvals, planning, and recurring operations.

## When to use this guide

Use this guide when you need to:

- Record a decision after a review or meeting.
- Explain why one option was chosen over another.
- Keep a clear history for future readers.
- Share a decision with people who were not present.
- Connect a decision to follow-up work.
- Prevent the same decision from being reopened without new information.
- Document tradeoffs before work begins.
- Summarize approvals, blockers, or changes in direction.

A decision record is useful when the decision may affect future work, timing, scope, ownership, quality, or expectations.

## Keep the decision easy to find

A decision record should be stored where people will naturally look for it.

Good locations include:

- A project folder.
- A shared planning document.
- A meeting notes folder.
- A review or approval record.
- A project summary.
- A change log.
- A task tracker.

Use a clear title that includes the decision topic.

Examples:

- Decision record: April report approval process
- Decision record: Export format for monthly summaries
- Decision record: Review deadline for customer files
- Decision record: Data cleanup approach

Avoid vague titles such as final decision, notes, update, or discussion.

## Start with the decision

Put the actual decision near the top. Readers should not have to read the full background before understanding the outcome.

Use a direct sentence:

- We will use the reviewed version as the final version.
- The report will be shared as a PDF instead of an editable document.
- The duplicate rows will be removed before the file is sent.
- The deadline will move from Thursday to Friday.
- The team will review the summary weekly instead of daily.

If no decision was made, say that clearly and record what is needed next.

## Include the reason

A decision record should explain the reason in plain language.

Useful reasons include:

- The option reduces confusion.
- The format is easier to review.
- The deadline gives reviewers enough time.
- The source data is incomplete.
- The old process caused duplicate work.
- The chosen option is easier to maintain.
- The decision matches the audience's needs.

Keep the reason short. A decision record should explain the logic, not repeat the entire discussion.

## Record the options considered

When there were several possible choices, list the main options.

Example:

Options considered:

- Share the file as an editable spreadsheet.
- Share the file as a PDF.
- Share both formats.

Decision:
Share both formats. The spreadsheet supports internal review, and the PDF gives external readers a stable version.

You do not need to include every minor variation. Include enough detail for a future reader to understand the tradeoff.

## Name the owner

Every decision should have an owner or responsible group.

The owner may be responsible for:

- Completing follow-up work.
- Updating the related file.
- Communicating the decision.
- Confirming approval.
- Reviewing the decision later.

Examples:

- Owner: Project lead
- Owner: Reporting team
- Owner: Document reviewer
- Owner: Operations coordinator

Avoid assigning ownership to everyone. Shared ownership often means unclear ownership.

## Include the date

Dates help readers understand when the decision was made and whether it may need review.

Use one consistent date format throughout the record.

Examples:

- Decision date: 2026-04-08
- Decision date: April 8, 2026
- Review date: April 15, 2026

If the decision expires or should be revisited, include a review date.

## Connect the decision to follow-up work

A decision record should make the next step clear.

Examples:

- Update the summary document by Friday.
- Send the PDF after final approval.
- Add the new columns before the next export.
- Confirm the remaining open item with the reviewer.
- Move the deferred item into the next planning cycle.

If no follow-up is needed, write: No follow-up required.

## Separate decision from discussion

A decision record is not a transcript. Keep discussion notes separate unless they help explain the result.

Include:

- The decision.
- The reason.
- Main options considered.
- The owner.
- Follow-up work.
- Open questions.

Avoid:

- Long message history.
- Repeated opinions.
- Side conversations.
- Unresolved comments that do not affect the decision.
- Private or sensitive details that are not needed.

## Use clear statuses

A decision record may have a status so readers know whether it is final.

Common statuses include:

- Proposed: A recommendation exists, but approval is still needed.
- Approved: The decision is accepted and ready to use.
- Deferred: The decision is delayed until more information is available.
- Replaced: A newer decision has taken its place.
- Cancelled: The decision is no longer needed.

Do not mark a decision approved unless the required reviewer or owner has approved it.

## Update replaced decisions

When a decision changes, do not delete the old record unless your process requires it. Update it so future readers can see what happened.

Example:

Status: Replaced
Replaced by: Decision record dated April 15, 2026
Reason: New source data changed the reporting timeline.

This keeps the decision history clear and prevents confusion when old links or references are still in use.

## Protect private information

Decision records are often copied into summaries, updates, and shared documents.

Avoid including:

- Passwords or access tokens.
- Private customer details unless required and approved.
- Sensitive personal information.
- Internal notes that should not be shared.
- Links that readers should not be able to access.
- Message history that is unrelated to the decision.

If sensitive context is necessary, summarize the safe reason and store the detailed material in the appropriate secure location.

## Decision record template

Use this template for a simple decision record.

Decision title:
[Short title]

Status:
[Proposed, approved, deferred, replaced, or cancelled]

Decision date:
[Date]

Owner:
[Person or team responsible]

Decision:
[One or two sentences stating what was decided]

Reason:
[Short explanation of why this decision was made]

Options considered:
- [Option 1]
- [Option 2]
- [Option 3]

Follow-up:
- [Action, owner, and date]
- [Action, owner, and date]

Open questions:
- [Question, or write None]

Related materials:
- [Link or reference, if safe to share]

## Example decision record

Decision title:
Monthly summary export format

Status:
Approved

Decision date:
April 8, 2026

Owner:
Reporting team

Decision:
The monthly summary will be shared as both a spreadsheet and a PDF. The spreadsheet will be used for internal review, and the PDF will be used for external readers.

Reason:
Reviewers need an editable version to check formulas and source data. External readers need a stable version that preserves formatting.

Options considered:
- Share only the spreadsheet.
- Share only the PDF.
- Share both formats.

Follow-up:
- Prepare the spreadsheet for internal review by April 10.
- Export the approved PDF after final review.
- Add the export date to the change log.

Open questions:
None.

Related materials:
- Monthly summary draft
- Review comments
- Change log

## Quick checklist

Before sharing a decision record, confirm that:

- The decision is stated near the top.
- The status is clear.
- The owner is named.
- The date is included.
- The reason is understandable.
- Main options are summarized.
- Follow-up work is listed.
- Open questions are separated from approved decisions.
- Replaced decisions link to the newer record.
- Private information is not exposed.

## Key takeaway

A useful decision record makes the outcome easy to understand later. State the decision clearly, explain the reason, name the owner, include dates, and connect the decision to follow-up work.
